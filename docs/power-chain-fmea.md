`V5_AUX` safety chain (`U8` LM393, `Q2`/`Q3`/`Q4`), telemetry. Board = `hardware/pcb-mods/nova_pcb_v6_power_v2/`,
copper frozen, so each row has a v6 bodge and a v7 fix. Nets come from a `kicad-cli sch export netlist`

## Top 3 before the first pack connect

1. **PC-01:** rework the 3 gate-network ends onto `Q1`.3, then pass the 5 V reversed-input test. Until then, the XT60 key is the only reverse-polarity protection.
2. **PC-05 + PC-02:** on the bench PSU, prove the E-stop takes every buck VOUT to 0 (ENA/ENB settled), and read each module's VOUT before any servo is attached.
3. **PC-11/PC-07:** run the LVC sweep both ways with a series resistor standing in for pack IR, and know whether the hardcut latches or cycles *before* a real pack gets to 12.4 V.

## What I'd tell an interviewer

- My reverse-polarity FET passed every continuity check and still didn't protect anything. The symbol's pin 2 was labelled source, but the part's pin 2 is the drain, so the gate clamp was referenced to the wrong terminal, and a reversed pack forward-biases the zener and turns the FET *on*. I found it by reading the exported netlist against the datasheet pinout, and the fix is three rework wires.
- A single-point failure hid in my safety chain: the E-stop and the undervoltage cut both depend on the 5 V UBEC for gate bias. If the UBEC dies, the bucks re-enable. On v7 the chain is biased from the pack and the enables default off, so losing logic power means losing motor power.
- I stopped trusting documented numbers the tool didn't compute. The trip points left out their own hysteresis resistors, the inrush energy was summed over capacitors that sit behind the bucks, not on the hot-plugged rail, and the "soft-start" never carried the inrush, because the body diode does. Each row here has a bench check with a pass criterion, so every claim ends up as a measurement.
