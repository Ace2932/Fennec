## #46 front-sensor crash protection — BANKED (2026-07-10)

**Status: BANKED after exhaustive exploration. Do NOT re-attempt from scratch.** Tried 5 forms — (1) organic downloaded fox mask, (2) geometric hard-surface fox guard, (3) head-mounted crash guard, (4) front-shoulder cantilever bumper, (5) triangulated keel — the chassis fights every one.

**ROOT FINDING (why it can't work on this chassis):**
- The forward-fall catch zone is ~(x195, z−35) — forward + low, to catch the ground ~15–19° of pitch before the head/D456 (pivot = front feet at (141, −169); head catches at ~83–85° pitch).
- **The entire forward-low volume (x64–160, z<70) IS the leg haa-sweep operating envelope** — no solid material can live there (shoulder.scad: "nothing below z+23.5 between the wheel/horn planes"). So there is NO anchor near the catch zone.
- The only forward solid is the shoulder **deck front edge** (z73–79, 6mm, mostly lightening vent) → a single-point mount → the ~120mm-out nose levers ~48 N·m onto a thin edge (marginal).
- Triangulating it (the structurally-correct fix) needs a **PA6-CF keel spanning ~⅓ the robot** from the deck, forward-down to the nose, then back through the leg-gap *over the battery* to a floor-plate/trunk-front tongue. It gates clean (moment → 653 N/anchor couple, catches 19° early, clears legs 7.4mm + ground 3.2mm) but is an absurd structure for a bumper. **This is the honest signal: the chassis can't host it.**
- **Head-mounted guards don't work either** — the D456 looks forward + 27° down (87°H×58°V), so the entire center-forward is view; any guard there blocks vision (verified: fox guard blocked it; the skeletal roll-bar was FoV-clean but structurally a weak cantilever + ugly).

**DECISION:** rely on **software fall-recovery** (like Unitree Go2 / Spot — they ship no front bumper either; fall damage isn't even warranted) + the inherent front-leg catch (front knee catches ~1° before the head — marginal but present). **Revisit ONLY with a v7 chassis** if front sensor protection becomes a hard requirement (would need a chassis with a solid forward-low mount designed in).

**LEARNINGS captured** (if ever revisited): fall geometry (catch angle <75° from the front-feet pivot); TPU honeycomb crush core ≈ 0.9 J/g SEA, hex cells, 0.5mm walls, progressive-collapse gradient, reusable/springs-back (refs in that session's research); the fox aesthetic fundamentally clashes with the industrial robot (organic vs machine — even chunky low-poly reads as "creature glued on").
